# BridgingMinds: market research and a position worth testing

**Research date: 1 November 2026 · Initial audience: students and early-career adults in Singapore**

BridgingMinds is the working product name in this report; the current application is branded MindGarden. This report recommends a direction, not a rename or an implementation change. It expands and revises the [30 October competitive review](../competitive-positioning.md).

## 1. The decision

**BridgingMinds has a coherent practice experience, but it does not yet have an established, defensible unique selling proposition.** The market already offers short speaking prompts, AI challenges, reasoning exercises, personal coaching, progress histories, gamification and supportive design. Even briefing-to-speaking exercises and transparent scoring limitations have close precedents.

The most promising position to test is:

> **Practise explaining and defending your own ideas before the conversation that matters.**

Start with Singapore adults aged 18+ moving from education into work: students explaining a project recommendation, interns answering a follow-up question, and new hires contributing a reasoned view in a meeting. The useful outcome is specific: a person can state a position, support it and respond thoughtfully when challenged.

The proposed product is a short preparation routine built around the learner's own response. Reading supplies material; AI supplies feedback and questions; a friend can supply a real challenge; the garden acknowledges participation. The experience should make it easy to see what the learner changed and, eventually, what they can do on a fresh task without help.

**This is a focused positioning hypothesis, not proof of an empty category or superior learning.** Local relevance, convenience and trustworthy execution can win customers without inventing a new instructional method. A durable advantage would have to be earned through useful practice, credible evaluation and access to a particular community.

## 2. What this research establishes

The review combines current official product pages, Singapore App Store listings, institutional provision, Singapore employer/education sources, original research and inspection of the local app. It includes direct speaking competitors, reasoning tools, reading products, general AI, human practice groups and habit apps.

The evidence has three distinct levels:

| Evidence | What it supports | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official product pages and store listings | Advertised capabilities, positioning and listed offers | Feature quality, actual purchases, market share or educational effectiveness |
| Surveys, reports and original studies | Findings for the stated population, period and intervention | Demand for BridgingMinds or transfer of another intervention's results to this app |
| Local implementation inspection | What the current app implements | Production readiness, assessment validity or product-market fit |

No customer interviews, paid competitor walkthroughs, subscriptions, outreach or comparative product trials were conducted. Undated pages are snapshots accessed on the research date. A feature not established in the reviewed pages is **not proven absent**. Detailed sources, uncertainties and pricing qualifications are retained in the supporting notes.

## 3. Singapore: a relevant problem, with substantial existing provision

Communication and reasoning matter to employers. SkillsFuture's 2023/24 report, using Singapore job postings from 2012–2022, places communication first among its five leading Critical Core Skills in the selected comparison years. More recently, SBF's survey of 511 businesses in July–August 2026 found 47% prioritising analytical/conceptual/evaluative/problem-solving skills. Respondents also cited training cost and staff-cover constraints. These support investigating short, useful practice; they do not measure consumer willingness to pay. [SkillsFuture report](https://www.skillsfuture.gov.sg/docs/default-source/skills-report-2023/ssg_2023_10nov2023_sdfe_final-%283%29.pdf), [SBF report](https://www.sbf.org.sg/docs/default-source/about-us/sbf-national-business-survey-2026-manpower-wages-supplement.pdf?sfvrsn=6599a99c_1)

The difficult competitive fact is that students already have alternatives. NUS provides free VMock Interview access; NTU combines VMock with coaching and mock-interview support; SMU publishes a SMART Interview guide. A generic interview bot would enter an already supplied use case. Availability does not tell us whether students use or like those tools, so discovery should examine actual behaviour. [NUS](https://www.nus.edu.sg/cfg/students/career-resources/ace-your-job-interview), [NTU](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/education/career-guidance-industry-collaborations/for-students/career-coaching---consultation), [SMU guide](https://careerservices.smu.edu.sg/sites/careerservices.smu.edu.sg/files/2025-12/171225_SMART%20Interview%20Guide_Singapore%20Management%20University.pdf)

There is also a plausible bridge from reading to expression: NLB's 2024 adult study found 89% of respondents read for leisure more than weekly, including news and online articles. That is not a book-reading-only statistic or evidence people want to complete exercises afterward. News should be a useful source of practice material, rather than the entire commercial proposition. [NLB study](https://www.nlb.gov.sg/main/about-us/press-room-and-publications/media-releases/2025/-/media/DFD163F34BC24D92BF3EB687D68A063E.ashx)

### Choose by situation, not just age

| Initial segment | Concrete situation | Commercial hypothesis | Main challenge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tertiary students, 18+, approaching internships or work | Explain a project decision; answer questions in a presentation or group discussion | Time-limited practice pack or sponsored cohort, reached through societies and peer programmes | Free institutional provision, temporary need and limited willingness to pay |
| Early-career adults, approximately 0–3 years in work | Recommend an action, explain a trade-off or disagree constructively in a meeting | Individual payment if useful repeatedly; community or coach referrals first | Existing AI tools, limited time and overly academic prompts |
| Immediate interview candidates | Explain an experience and handle follow-up questions | Event-based access | Strong direct competition; use may end after the interview |
| Curious readers | Form a view and discuss an article | Later retention activity or reading-group extension | Interest in content may never become demand for practice |

**Recommendation:** run separate student and early-career pilots, then concentrate on the segment demonstrating independent return and payment. Recruit people with a real discussion or presentation within 2–4 weeks. Include adult participants from different tertiary pathways and work contexts; do not infer a national communication deficit or treat a local accent as a problem.

### Claims to retire

- “Other apps only count fillers.” Several explicitly coach structure, content and contextual responses.
- “Personal AI memory is our moat.” Competitors already advertise it.
- “The first reading-to-speaking app.” Wellspoken provides a direct counterexample.
- “Scientifically proven evaluation.” A research-informed rubric does not validate this LLM evaluator.
- “The garden proves growth.” Resources currently reflect participation, not demonstrated skill.

## 5. What the app has today—and what is still a proposal

The app's strongest current asset is its connected practice sequence, especially optional guidance and preservation of the learner's work. It has more substance than a prompt plus a score, but several proposed advantages are not implemented.

| Capability | Current state | Strategic significance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speaking/writing, AI debate and optional self-reflection | Implemented | Useful entry points; broad competitive overlap |
| Three guiding questions, hints, linked retries and exact earlier/current text | Implemented | Can support learner-authored revision; value and quality need comparison |
| Annotated AI model response | Explicitly requested, separate from the saved learner response | Useful illustration; does not demonstrate the learner has acquired the skill |
| Private friend debate | Asynchronous text/recorded turns | Test whether a real listener makes practice more useful; coordination may reduce completion |
| Curated reading response | Six readings with bounded source context | Starting material for a pilot, not a broad current-affairs curriculum |
| Today's news | CNA/NPR/NASA headlines and publisher links, separate from assessed reading | Not yet a complete news → response → source-grounded feedback workflow |
| Audio assessment | Recording/transcription; feedback assesses corrected text | No verified pronunciation detection, acoustic filler analysis or delivery assessment |
| Garden | Effort-based resources, plants, archive/replant, 48-hour watering deadline | Habit mechanic to test; care obligations may also discourage return |
| Adaptive curriculum and delayed transfer | Not implemented | Possible next experiment, not an existing personalized learning system |
| Validation and public release | Local backend; evaluation is provisional; production work remains | Working local flows do not establish accurate assessment or launch readiness |

## 6. A stronger experience to test

### One repeatable unit: prepare a reasoned response

Aim for a brief routine; measure its actual duration before advertising a five-minute completion promise:

1. **Choose a relevant situation.** An authored scenario or appropriately licensed short source gives the user something concrete to respond to.
2. **Make an original attempt.** Speak or type a position in the user's own words.
3. **Address one useful challenge.** Feedback identifies a specific gap with supporting text. Optional guiding questions help; users can proceed directly when ready.
4. **Revise.** Preserve both versions. Keep any AI-authored draft visibly separate.
5. **Apply the same skill later.** Offer a fresh situation without the earlier model answer. This transfer check is proposed work, not a current feature.

A possible task is explaining why a project team should choose one solution, then answering a question about the trade-off. Another is discussing a Singapore issue from a bounded source and distinguishing what the source establishes from the user's own inference. These are proposed content directions, not assumptions that every user needs the same argument template.

The interface can stay minimal: one task, one response, one useful next action. Put methodology, transcript controls and explanations behind clearly labelled disclosures. Preserve optional reflection; forced journaling could become friction for someone preparing an immediate conversation.

### What would make this worth choosing?

| Candidate advantage | What would count as evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Less setup than general AI | Users reach useful feedback and a revision faster, with fewer abandoned sessions |
| Useful local situations | Participants choose the app for an actual upcoming task after seeing alternatives |
| Feedback they can inspect and correct | Human review finds appropriate interpretations; users can identify and dispute errors |
| Better follow-through | Learners voluntarily revisit a response and practise on another day |
| Skills that transfer | Blinded human ratings improve on new, unaided tasks against an appropriate comparator |
| A useful human layer | Invited partners both contribute and return; users value the exchange beyond solo AI practice |

None is proven today. Local topics alone are easy to copy; a saved history alone is not a curriculum. More durable assets could include carefully reviewed practice material, consented and human-labelled evaluation cases, reliable correction workflows and repeatable community distribution. Their value depends on quality and adoption, not simply accumulating recordings.

### Pronunciation and fillers: a later supporting layer

The current app does **not** extract mispronounced words or provide verified acoustic filler detection. Adding these would mainly close a feature gap with existing speech coaches. Prioritise the thinking-and-response routine first.

If pilot users need audio coaching, test a small optional module with playable timestamps, uncertainty and a clear distinction between transcription mistakes and learner mistakes. Avoid treating zero fillers or a particular accent as the goal. The Council of Europe's guidance prioritises intelligibility; that is a useful design principle, not validation of a new scoring model. [Phonological competence](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/phonological-competence)

## 7. Pricing, distribution and commercial viability

### Observed local price anchors

These are Singapore iOS purchase listings checked on the research date, not guaranteed new-user checkout offers. Promotions, entitlements, platform and eligibility can differ.

| Product | Published Singapore entries | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wellspoken | S$19.98 monthly; annual entries from S$49.98 to S$129.98 | Multiple annual SKUs; do not assume the lowest is universally available. [Listing](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/wellspoken-articulation-coach/id6752822613) |
| Orai | S$14.98 monthly; S$69.98 yearly | A relevant paid consumer benchmark. [Listing](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/orai-improve-public-speaking/id1203178170) |
| Speeko | Full Access S$34.98 monthly / S$149.98 annually; a distinct annual entry at S$99.98 | Offer names differ; access equivalence is unresolved. [Listing](https://apps.apple.com/sg/app/speeko-ai-for-public-speaking/id1071468459) |
| Free alternatives | Kialo Edu; institution-provided VMock for eligible NUS users | The real comparison price can be zero. [Kialo funding](https://www.kialo-edu.com/pricing-why-kialo-is-free), [NUS](https://www.nus.edu.sg/cfg/students/career-resources/ace-your-job-interview) |

There is no applicable willingness-to-pay finding for BridgingMinds. Test **S$8 versus S$16 for a two-week app-only practice pack** as deliberately provisional offers after users experience it. These are research hypotheses, not an optimal price or evidence of affordability. A small pilot cannot estimate precise price elasticity.

Choose the business model from behaviour:

- **Event pack:** useful if people practise around an interview or presentation and stop afterward.
- **Subscription:** plausible only if separate work or study situations generate repeated value.
- **Sponsored cohort:** plausible if an organiser with a real budget pays for a defined outcome. Free participation is not evidence of institutional demand.

Start through student societies, peer mentoring, alumni/early-career communities and independent coaches. Offer a compact, voluntary pilot tied to a real event. Campus licensing and employer programmes can follow evidence; they introduce administration, existing contracts and buyer requirements the current app does not yet address. No organisation has agreed to a partnership.

Avoid a headline TAM assembled from student counts or job vacancies. Build a reachable-market model from **organisers willing to invite → eligible participants reached → activated users → independent return → paid continuation**. Track net revenue after platform fees, transcription/evaluation costs, retries, support and refunds. The missing inputs are distribution and paid behaviour, not a large population number.

## 8. A six-week validation plan

All thresholds below are proposed operating gates to prevent moving the goalposts. They are not industry benchmarks, statistical proof or evidence already collected.

| Timing | Work | Decision evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | Interview 12 students, 12 early-career adults and 4–6 organisers/buyers. Ask about a recent real event, existing tools and effort spent. Recruit beyond friends. | A specific recurring problem; an accessible recruitment channel; users dissatisfied with a concrete part of their current routine |
| Weeks 2–3 | Run separate 15–20-person cohorts. Compare with each participant's actual alternative and a well-prepared general-AI workflow on comparable tasks; counterbalance order. | First useful session, self-authored revisions, time/friction, preference after comparison, independent return and feedback errors |
| Weeks 4–6 | Offer paid continuation and observe use after payment. Separately test a small sponsored cohort with a named buyer, budget and date. | Payments among all eligible offers, refunds, continuing practice and cost to serve; genuine buyer commitment |

For a 20-person cohort, a provisional gate is **12 completing an original response and meaningful feedback interaction**, and **8 completing a second response or meaningful revision on a different day by day 14 without personal chasing**. Opening the app or watering plants does not count toward that return gate. Report guide use, self-authored revision and reminder-driven use separately. For commercial discovery, seek at least five independent paying continuations in the stronger segment with useful subsequent practice. Five purchases do not establish product-market fit.

Track friend invitations through both participants responding and returning; an invite alone is not social engagement. Ask whether the garden encourages meaningful practice or only watering. The current 48-hour plant death mechanic was requested and is implemented, but its effect on this audience is unknown. Test the care burden before making retention claims or expanding the shop.

**Stop or change direction** if participants consistently prefer their existing alternative, cannot identify useful feedback, return only after founder reminders, mainly request AI-written answers, or do not value a second real task. Student engagement without personal payment justifies testing sponsorship, not declaring B2B demand. Episodic paid use may justify a pack even if a subscription fails.

After a positive six-week result, spend the next six weeks improving the winning task family, feedback reliability and repeatable recruitment. Add roughly ten reviewed scenarios before a large content library. Implement only the next-practice/transfer experiment the evidence calls for. Defer pronunciation scoring, broad social feeds, leaderboards and a larger garden economy until they address an observed barrier.

## 9. Proof, content and naming issues that affect the strategy

**Learning claims require a different study from retention.** Gamification research reports average benefits across varied interventions; it does not validate this garden. A controlled mathematics study also illustrates why better AI-assisted work must be distinguished from later unaided performance. Neither supplies a causal result for Singapore speaking practice. Use unseen tasks, blinded human raters, suitable comparisons and delayed measurement; test evaluator agreement separately from educational benefit. [Gamification meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w), [Bastani et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), [existing assessment plan](../../assessment-benchmark.md)

**CNA is not automatically a commercial content licence.** Its RSS page describes personal, non-commercial use. Resolve permission and the scope of use for the existing headline feed before commercial launch, as well as for any future CNA-derived practice material. A practical alternative to investigate is original briefs using appropriately licensed material; Singapore's Open Data Licence allows covered dataset reuse subject to its conditions and attribution, not unrestricted reuse of every government webpage or third-party article. [CNA RSS](https://www.channelnewsasia.com/rss), [Singapore Open Data Licence](https://data.gov.sg/open-data-licence)

**The working name already overlaps with a Singapore training business.** BridgingMinds Network publicly offers corporate learning and training in Singapore. If this is unrelated to your organisation, resolve naming and discoverability before substantial launch spending. This is an observed market overlap, not a trademark determination. MindGarden also has existing uses, so it should not be assumed to be a cleared replacement. [BridgingMinds Network](https://www.bridgingminds.net/), [Mind Garden assessment publisher](https://www.mindgarden.com/), [Mind Garden app](https://www.mind-garden.app/)

**Pilot readiness is separate from product direction.** The local implementation still needs the applicable hosting, account recovery, data handling, device/accessibility and evaluator-quality work before a public pilot. AI credentials alone do not complete those tasks. This research changes no application code or deployment.

## 10. Recommended positioning to carry into interviews

**Audience:** Singapore students and early-career adults preparing to explain a view in a real conversation.

**Promise:** “Practise explaining and defending your own ideas before the conversation that matters.”

**Demonstration:** an original response → one useful challenge → the learner's revision → optional discussion with a friend. Show the actual saved words. Describe later independent practice as planned until it exists.

**Reason to return:** another relevant situation and a useful next practice, with the garden as optional encouragement.

**Reason to believe today:** inspectable response history and clear assessment boundaries. **Reason to believe later:** measured feedback quality, independent performance and repeat use by the intended audience.

The next investment should be in proving this routine useful to a narrow group. A longer feature list would make the app broader without establishing why someone should choose it.
