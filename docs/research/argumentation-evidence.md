# Argumentation and critical-thinking assessment evidence

Research checked 2026-10-30. This document distinguishes empirical findings, conceptual frameworks, and recommendations for MindGarden. It does not establish that the app, an LLM, or its GROW levels are scientifically validated. Existing implementation context: `docs/grow-rubric.md`.

## Recommended foundation

Use an original, task-specific analytic coaching rubric: argument **support, inference, clarity, and engagement with relevant alternatives**. The strongest conceptual fit for short everyday arguments is the acceptability/relevance/sufficiency account synthesized by Wachsmuth et al.; Toulmin supplies a useful distinction between a reason and the warrant connecting it to a claim. ASRAW supplies relevant empirical work on assessing support and rebuttals, but its essay weights should not be transplanted into brief responses.

Keep GROW only as a friendly presentation layer, with separate versions and labels for each mode. Assess the submitted performance within its task and available evidence. Do not infer general intelligence, disposition, political correctness, or a person's enduring ability. Source counts, length, sophisticated vocabulary, confidence of delivery, and agreement with the evaluator are not substitutes for reasoning quality. A concise answer can satisfy a criterion fully.

This is a **research-informed adaptation**, not adoption of a validated instrument. Validity concerns particular interpretations and uses: an established instrument's evidence does not automatically validate a new task, population, scoring model, or consequence. This principle is explicit in [AERA/APA/NCME Standards, Standards 1.0–1.3](https://www.testingstandards.net/uploads/7/6/6/4/76643089/standards_2014edition.pdf). [Kane (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1111/jedm.12000) likewise distinguishes validating a score interpretation from validating its use.

## Primary evidence and its limits

### Toulmin: useful structure theory, not a psychometric test

Toulmin's *The Uses of Argument* (1958; updated 2003) analyzes how arguments support conclusions; [the publisher's chapter “The Layout of Arguments”](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511840005.007) establishes the original theoretical source. It is not a standardized performance instrument with a validation sample or reliability coefficient. **App recommendation:** use claim–support–warrant relationships as analysis aids, not a checklist whose element count determines quality. Do not claim that using Toulmin makes automated scores validated. Book wording and diagrams have no verified open commercial adaptation permission in this review; use independently written explanations and citation.

### Stapleton & Wu (2015): structure is insufficient

[Primary institutional abstract](https://repository.eduhk.hk/en/publications/assessing-the-quality-of-arguments-in-students-persuasive-writing-7/), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeap.2014.11.006): 125 Hong Kong high-school students produced argumentative essays; six structurally exemplary essays were selected, and 46 doctoral students rated 20 commonly used reasons. Strong surface structure could coexist with weak substance. The resulting Analytic Scoring Rubric for Argumentative Writing (ASRAW) was offered as a teaching guide. This case study supports examining the quality of reasons, not merely the presence of argument components. It does not validate arbitrary short-answer or LLM scores. The original full-text reliability estimates were not verified here and are not reported. No open commercial license for its rubric wording/table was verified.

Terminology: searches did not establish an argument-assessment instrument named **AQME**. Do not silently substitute that acronym for ASRAW, the ArgQuality corpus, or another instrument.

### Landrieu et al. (2022): strong ASRAW agreement in a bounded setting

[Peer-reviewed full paper, Methods and Table 3](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2022.784261/full): 164 eleventh-graders wrote 45-minute, two-source Dutch essays; 130 argumentative texts entered analysis, averaging 401 words. Two analytic/holistic raters included a researcher and a teacher given 3–4 hours' training. Only 31 texts were double-coded. ASRAW total-score consistency ICC was **.98**; holistic ICC **.48**. Units were indicated beforehand, making analytic scoring easier. The **.83** figure is scale-separation reliability of a separate pairwise-comparison procedure with 132 student raters, not ASRAW reliability. These metrics are not interchangeable. The study supports structured human scoring under these conditions; it cannot establish app generalization or AI agreement. Its article is CC BY; that does not establish permission for third-party ASRAW wording from the original publication.

### Wachsmuth et al. (2017): closest argument-quality taxonomy, modest agreement

[EACL peer-reviewed paper](https://aclanthology.org/E17-1017/), [PDF, §§3–4 and Table 3](https://aclanthology.org/E17-1017.pdf): taxonomy distinguishes logical cogency, rhetorical effectiveness, and dialectical reasonableness. Cogency comprises acceptable, relevant, sufficient premises. Three experts annotated 320 short debate-portal arguments; 304 were unanimously classified argumentative. Table 3's Krippendorff α is explicitly for the **most agreeing pair**: cogency **.44**, overall quality **.51**, global sufficiency **.27**. These are not all-rater reliability estimates. Full three-rater agreement ranged **17.4–44.7%** across dimensions. The initial seven-rater pilot's α was at most **.22**. This is theory-informed taxonomy and corpus evidence, not a validated general intelligence test or guaranteed automated scorer. **App recommendation:** use logical support and context-sensitive engagement; do not reward emotional appeal or audience persuasion as proof of sound reasoning. Corpus reuse rights must be checked separately from paper access; no dataset import is proposed.

### AAC&U Critical Thinking VALUE: educational assessment framework

The [original rubric reproduced by Bethune-Cookman University](https://www.cookman.edu/ie/_files/all_rubrics-aacu.pdf) describes faculty-developed criteria for institutional assessment, explicitly distinct from grading individual assignments. It covers issue explanation, evidence, context/assumptions, position, and conclusions. It is a useful content-coverage reference, but its undergraduate achievement descriptors are not automatically suitable for one brief spontaneous answer. AAC&U's [official VALUE glossary](https://valuesupport.aacu.org/support/solutions/articles/48000269306-value-institute-glossary) describes certified scorers and an unscorable outcome.

[Heinrich et al. (2015), peer-reviewed full paper, Methods](https://doi.org/10.1177/1053825915592890) studied four sustainability-learning settings. Three researchers coded 339 artifacts using a substantially elaborated, iteratively revised codebook derived from VALUE. Reported reliability includes Krippendorff α **.92** and Cohen κ **.97**, depending on comparison. Those values belong to the study's coding procedure, not an untouched universal VALUE rubric. The authors avoided statistical comparisons between courses because their contexts differed. This supports local adaptation and calibration, not automatic comparability across prompts.

Current AAC&U permission pages returned HTTP403 in this review. An older AAC&U webpage reproduced in a [2018–19 university report](https://www.csus.edu/academic-affairs/program-assessment/_internal/_documents/1819-report-pdfs-by-clge/ssis/1819-ba-liberal-studies-full1.pdf) required prior permission for commercial software and unchanged complete rubrics. Treat this as historical policy evidence, not confirmation of current terms. Do not embed copied VALUE descriptors commercially without confirming current permission.

### Facione: separate Delphi consensus from CCTST testing evidence

The [1990 Delphi report](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED315423.pdf) synthesizes **46 experts' consensus**, distinguishing cognitive skills from dispositions. Its interpretation, analysis, evaluation, inference, explanation and self-regulation concepts help define the construct. Consensus is content/theory evidence; it is not an empirical accuracy or reliability test of an app.

The [CCTST 1990 Technical Report #1](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED327549.pdf) is an author-produced technical report, not a peer-reviewed journal trial. It describes four college studies involving **1,169 students**, using a standardized multiple-choice instrument. The first experiment reports KR-20 internal consistency **.69** pretest and **.68** posttest (printed p.13); these are historical instrument estimates, not human-rater agreement and not current-product coefficients. Course-related score differences do not establish transfer to everyday debate. Current [publisher information](https://insightassessment.com/products/) identifies CCTST as calibrated for college populations. **App recommendation:** a properly licensed external instrument could be one convergent comparator in a later study. Do not copy proprietary items or imply that GROW inherits CCTST validation.

### Ennis–Weir: open-response test, specific task and scoring rules

The [author's manual/supplement](https://criticalthinking.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Ennis-Weir-and-Supplement.pdf), Table2, reports inter-rater coefficients including **.86** for 27 college students and **.82** for 28 gifted eighth-graders; other samples vary. The supplement explicitly distinguishes consistency from successfully measuring the intended construct and cautions about nonstandard contexts and language differences. These are author-reported studies of a particular essay test, not arbitrary prompts. The [author's assessment page](https://criticalthinking.net/test/) permits no-cost download and photocopying the final three test pages. This is not verified permission to commercially repurpose its scoring materials as a general API. **App recommendation:** possible external validation reference under appropriate terms, not the everyday coaching engine.

### ICTET-A (2024): a newer structured open-response alternative

[Hollis et al., peer-reviewed paper](https://doi.org/10.5840/inquiryct20241513), [full PDF](https://www.pdcnet.org/collection/fshow?file_type=pdf&id=inquiryct_2024_0999_1_5_13&pdfname=inquiryct_Hollis.pdf): 100 self-selected adults answered nine questions about one text, approximately 30 minutes; 91 held undergraduate/postgraduate degrees. Three graders' total-score agreement ICC was **.865** (95%CI **.672–.933**), consistency ICC **.918**; correlation with Ennis–Weir was Spearman **.78**. One item's agreement was poor (**.441**). These provide bounded reliability/convergent evidence, not representative norms, multi-topic generalization, or LLM validation. The article is **CC BY-NC-ND**, so public access does not permit commercial adaptation of its protected material. This test is closer to structured reading analysis than to a two-turn debate, but would require a distinct task and permissions review.

## Proposed app adaptation — recommendations, not study findings

| Mode | Assessable performance | Required context and boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spin & Speak | Support, reasoning, clarity, relevant qualification | Judge the answer to the actual prompt. An illustrative example may support a limited claim; references are not mandatory. Do not require a counterargument if the question offers no reasonable opportunity. |
| Debate | Support, reasoning, clarity, engagement | Include role-labeled opponent turns and chronology. Score only the learner's answer; distinguish accurately answering an objection from merely mentioning an opposing view. Without an opponent or relevant challenge, engagement is N/A. |
| Read & Reflect | Understanding, interpretation, clarity, reflection | Judge against the exact provided summary and selected question. Do not claim full-article comprehension, general fact-checking, or verification of outside knowledge. Missing source context requires abstention from source-dependent judgments. |

For each applicable criterion, write original observable 1–4 anchors, with a separate N/A state. Higher levels should mean stronger fit between support and conclusion, not more words or citations. N/A must not become a low score. Store the framework, mode, model, template, evidence scope, and version with each result; preserve historical evaluations. Avoid aggregating ordinal dimensions into a purported calibrated ability score.

Local pattern matching should offer self-review prompts and exact saved excerpts, with **all numeric levels null**. A connective word cannot establish a valid inference; shared vocabulary cannot establish comprehension. Model judgments may be provisional rubric ratings, explicitly unvalidated. Model self-reported confidence is not a calibrated probability. Exact-span validation proves that quoted text exists, not that an interpretation is correct. These are engineering/measurement recommendations for this app, not published validation findings.

## What would support stronger claims later

Define the intended interpretation first: feedback on this response, or generalizable growth across tasks. Create varied, consented app-response samples by mode, topic, answer length, language background, and audio-transcription quality. Have trained independent humans score a held-out set, with both agreement and disagreements retained. Report criterion-level exact/adjacent agreement and an appropriate chance-adjusted ordinal statistic with uncertainty; distinguish absolute agreement from correlation and averaged-rater reliability. Test prompt/rater/model variability, concise strong responses, unsupported eloquence, counterargument fairness, source contradictions, and abstention decisions. Protect the test set from prompt tuning. Examine whether feedback improves independently rated later responses, not just retries on the same task.

A fixed 100–200-response pilot is a feasible starting proposal, not a universal power calculation or release threshold. Determine sample requirements for the chosen claims and subgroup precision with assessment expertise. Software tests, a rubric citation, a reliable human essay study, or a few agreeable LLM examples cannot replace this work.

## Access and scope notes

Only primary papers, author reports, official instruments, and identifiable reproductions of original documents underpin claims above. Some publisher/organization pages were inaccessible; absent coefficients or unverified permissions are deliberately left unresolved. This is a targeted evidence review, not a registered systematic review. It found no evidence establishing a universally validated framework for every unrestricted app response, nor validation of the proposed MindGarden adaptation. The commercial product should use original anchors and attribution, while any imported instrument, table, or corpus needs its own rights check.
