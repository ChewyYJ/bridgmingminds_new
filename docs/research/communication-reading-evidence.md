# Communication and reading: evidence for MindGarden feedback

Research checked 30 October 2026. This document distinguishes an established assessment's evidence from an adaptation for MindGarden. Recommendations and example anchors below are original design proposals, not validated scales or reproduced official rubrics.

## Recommendation

Use **task-specific, evidence-linked formative feedback**, not a language-proficiency or general-ability score. Spin & Speak can draw on communication research for understandable organisation and relevant development, alongside the argumentation framework researched separately. Read & Reflect should use the reading processes of **understanding, interpretation, and evaluation/reflection**, constrained by the particular question and supplied text. Neither mode should award CEFR, Cambridge, TOEFL, PISA, or AAC&U levels.

The reviewed sources provide stronger support for the *constructs to examine* than for the reliability of a generic LLM rating one short response. MindGarden's prompts, four-point anchors, model, source summaries, population and consequences constitute a new assessment use. They need their own validation. Until then, any LLM numbers are provisional descriptions of this response; the deterministic local fallback should offer questions and revision suggestions without numbers.

The present app evaluates text or corrected transcripts. An uploaded recording does not make delivery observable to a text evaluator. Keep delivery unassessed. For reading, the evaluator has a permitted summary, not necessarily the full publisher article: label and enforce that scope.

## Claim-to-source map and transfer limits

### CEFR Companion Volume (Council of Europe, 2020)

The revised phonological-control framework prioritises intelligibility, with separate attention to articulation and prosody. It rejects an idealised native accent as the goal. Appendix 6 reports a phonology qualitative phase of 250 participants and a quantitative phase of **272**, including descriptor-level judgements and learner-video judgements. This is descriptor-development/calibration evidence, not validation of an LLM's pronunciation rating. General descriptor project counts elsewhere in the volume must not be substituted for the phonology sample. Relevant locations: printed pp. 133–135 and 243–249. [Official Companion Volume](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4).

**MindGarden inference:** the communicative emphasis supports clear meaning over accent conformity. Coherence and development can inform original text criteria, but a one-minute answer is insufficient evidence for a person's CEFR proficiency. Phonological descriptors cannot be applied to a transcript. The dedicated revision report documents the rationale and process in greater detail. [Piccardo, 2016, Phonological Scale Revision Process Report](https://rm.coe.int/168073fff9).

### Cambridge English B2 First

Cambridge's speaking guide distinguishes grammar/vocabulary, discourse management, pronunciation and interactive communication. Different parts of the examination elicit different evidence, including an extended turn and interaction. These criteria concern the whole test, not only one answer. [Official B2 assessment guide](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/images/168619-assessing-speaking-performance-at-level-b2.pdf).

For its **2025** examinations, Cambridge publishes B2 First Speaking reliability of **0.95**, with a standard error of measurement of **2.05 Cambridge English Scale points**. Its explanation identifies the **Feldt test** for speaking and the contribution of two raters. No sample size is provided in that table. These are publisher-reported operational statistics for the complete assessment, not independent evidence for MindGarden or LLM-human agreement. [Quality and accountability, reliability tables](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/english-research-group/quality-and-accountability/).

**MindGarden inference:** relevance, understandable sequencing and responsiveness are useful observables. Do not equate discourse organisation with vocabulary sophistication, penalise an uncomplicated but sufficient answer, or infer interactive skill from a monologue.

### ETS speaking rubrics and automated scoring

The older TOEFL speaking rubric separates delivery, language use and topic development. This is a useful conceptual reminder that communication content and audio delivery are different evidence streams. [ETS speaking rubric PDF](https://www.ets.org/content/dam/ets-org/pdfs/toefl/toefl-ibt-speaking-rubrics.pdf).

**Version warning:** ETS changed TOEFL iBT on **21 January 2026**; its current speaking tasks include Listen and Repeat and Take an Interview. Older independent/integrated-task rubrics and six-task research must be identified as historical evidence, not presented as validation of the current format. The official page contains both old and new sections, so use its dated 2026 section. [Current ETS content and structure](https://www.ets.org/content/ets-org/language-master/in/home/toefl/institutions/ibt/about/content-structure.html).

Chen and colleagues' **SpeechRater 5.0** study trained on 667 test takers/4,002 responses and evaluated on 333/1,998. Table 15 reports item-level system–human correlation **r = .557** for 1,775 included responses versus human–human **r = .591** for 1,756. Table 16 reports six-response aggregate system–human **r = .770**, 264 speakers, versus human–human **r = .88**, 263 speakers. Inclusion rules explain the differing subsets. These are correlations, not exact agreement. The model combined acoustic and linguistic features and was not a general-purpose LLM. [Chen et al., 2018, ETS RR-18-10, Tables 15–16](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1202795.pdf), [official report record](https://www.ets.org/research/policy_research_reports/publications/report/2018/jyzm.html).

**MindGarden inference:** even a purpose-built calibrated system's single-response results differ materially from aggregates. A high correlation for a multi-task examination cannot justify a precise rating of one transcript.

A separate exploratory ETS oral-communication study used **eight native-English-speaking undergraduate interns**, four 2/4-minute presentation tasks and two raters (32 videos). Its all-task generalisability coefficient was **.83**, with absolute-decision coefficient **.77**; a single-task configuration reported **.59** and **.53**. This tiny, narrow sample supports caution about task sampling, not a universal rule that two tasks validate an assessment. [Roohr, Burkander and Mao, 2018, ETS RR-18-09, Tables 4–5](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1202759.pdf).

### AAC&U VALUE oral and written communication

The Oral Communication rubric is designed for sufficiently developed single-speaker presentations. Its framing explicitly cautions against applying it to an ordinary answer to one question. It considers organisation, supporting material, central message, language and delivery; delivery includes visible behaviours unavailable from a transcript. It was designed for institution-level discussion of undergraduate learning. **Wholesale use is a poor fit for Spin & Speak.** [Original AAC&U oral rubric, hosted by University of Toronto](https://teaching.utoronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/Rubric-AACU-Oral-Communication.pdf).

The Written Communication rubric emphasises the audience, purpose and context of a particular work sample. Its other dimensions concern development, genre conventions, source use, and language/mechanics. It explicitly encourages local adaptation. **MindGarden inference:** purposeful clarity is useful; academic citation conventions and polished essay structure should not become hidden requirements for an informal short reflection. [Original AAC&U collection, written rubric pp. 31–32, hosted by Dublin City University](https://www.dcu.ie/sites/default/files/inline-files/all_rubrics_0.pdf).

The 2017 *On Solid Ground* report describes the 2015–16 VALUE initiative across 92 institutions in 12 states, using student work late in degree programmes. About 20% of written-communication and critical-thinking work was double-scored. Its agreement analyses concern those particular rubrics and assessment procedures. They **do not validate the oral rubric, every VALUE rubric, or an LLM adaptation**; the report also cautions about representativeness. [McConnell and Rhodes, 2017, original report, pp. 29–32, university-hosted copy](https://explore.neumann.edu/hubfs/AA%20-%20Webpage%20Images/Mission-and-Ministry/nifs/resources/On%20Solid%20Ground.pdf?hsLang=en).

### OECD PISA reading

The 2018 framework distinguishes locating information, understanding, and evaluating/reflecting. It includes inference, assessing source quality and credibility, reflecting on content or form, and handling conflicts between sources. These processes support a task-sensitive reading-reflection design. PISA, however, is a standardised assessment of 15-year-olds across many items; the framework is not a validated four-level rubric for an adult's single journal response. Relevant chapter: reading literacy, especially the cognitive-process discussion. [OECD, 2019, PISA 2018 Assessment and Analytical Framework](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2019/04/pisa-2018-assessment-and-analytical-framework_d1c359c7/b25efab8-en.pdf).

PISA's operational evidence depends on item development, translation and quality assurance. Open responses are coded under structured procedures, including double coding used to examine within- and cross-country consistency. This is evidence about an assessment system, not an assurance that adopting process names makes a new instrument valid. [OECD, PISA 2018 Results, quality assurance](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2018-results-volume-ii_b5fd1b8f-en/full-report/component-21.html).

**MindGarden inference:** this is the strongest conceptual match for Read & Reflect. Judge an interpretation against the supplied summary and chosen question. Do not penalise the absence of source criticism when the task asks for a personal connection; do not require multiple sources when only one is provided. A plausible inference can go beyond an explicit sentence if its connection is explained. Faithful reporting of a supplied text does not prove that text's real-world claims true.

## What to assess in the app

These are original proposed observables, not official framework descriptors. The versioned runtime rubric should remain the single source of exact production anchors.

| Mode | Suggested observable | Evidence and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spin & Speak | Support | A relevant example, stated observation or reason that supports the person's claim. Do not require a formal citation for a short spontaneous argument. |
| Spin & Speak | Reasoning | An explained connection between support and conclusion, rather than counting linking words. |
| Spin & Speak | Clarity | A recoverable main point and an understandable sequence appropriate to the response length; sophisticated vocabulary is not required. |
| Spin & Speak | Nuance | A relevant condition, limitation or alternative when the prompt creates that opportunity. Not automatic credit for saying “however.” |
| Read & Reflect | Understanding | Faithful identification of the relevant supplied idea, without attributing unsupported details to the text. |
| Read & Reflect | Interpretation | A supported explanation or inference that answers the chosen question. |
| Read & Reflect | Clarity | A comprehensible connection between the supplied idea and the person's answer. |
| Read & Reflect | Reflection | A reasoned connection, implication, uncertainty or evaluation that the question actually invites. Source-credibility analysis only where information and task support it. |

An illustrative **original** progression for interpretation is: (1) a conclusion with no clear relation to the supplied idea; (2) a plausible connection whose explanation is incomplete; (3) a clear explanation grounded in a relevant supplied detail; (4) a well-supported explanation that handles a meaningful qualification within the question's scope. This is not a PISA proficiency scale. “Not enough evidence” must be separate from level 1, and a concise fully sufficient explanation may earn a higher description than a longer repetitive one.

Before the answer, disclose the applicable criteria. After it, show one useful revision target and evidence-linked details on demand. A general reflection prompt cannot silently become an examination of unasked skills.

### Source and observation contract

For each applicable criterion, preserve the response evidence span, short rationale and concrete revision. Distinguish:

- **Not elicited:** the prompt did not give a reasonable opportunity to demonstrate it.
- **Not observable:** required evidence, such as sound or another source, is unavailable.
- **Insufficient evidence:** the response does not support a dependable judgement.
- **Unverified outside claim:** the answer invokes a fact beyond the supplied material; this is not automatically a false claim.

The evaluator should receive the exact question, permitted source context, task limits and the same rubric shown in the UI. Separate respondent quotes from source quotes and retain source identifiers. Validate exact respondent offsets mechanically; this establishes quote integrity, not score validity. Requests to ignore criteria inside an article or response are task content, not evaluator instructions. Keep model/rubric/template/source versions with results. Model-reported confidence is uncalibrated certainty, not a probability of correctness.

## Audio, accent and access

Text evaluation should not report pronunciation, prosody, pacing, eye contact, confidence, personality or emotional state. Automatically inserted punctuation and ASR omissions can alter perceived organisation; allow users to inspect and correct transcripts before feedback. Do not punish the writer for transcription uncertainty.

In a historical study of five commercial speech-recognition systems, Koenecke and colleagues examined recordings from 73 Black and 42 White speakers and found average word-error rates of **.35** and **.19**, respectively. The finding concerns the tested systems, corpus and time; it does not measure today's configured transcription provider or justify assumptions about an individual speaker. It demonstrates why pipeline errors require an explicit fairness audit. [Koenecke et al., 2020, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7149386/).

A future audio-specific module would need consented raw audio, suitable tasks, human intelligibility judgements and validation across the intended English varieties, recording conditions and access needs. Assess whether a listener can recover meaning; do not use proximity to a native accent as a proxy. Keep it separate from content feedback so choosing text does not reduce a user's apparent capability. This is a design recommendation, not a claim that such an audio module is currently validated or implemented.

## Evidence required before stronger claims

Recommended MindGarden validation plan, rather than a finding from an imported rubric:

1. Review task/criterion alignment with communication and reading-assessment specialists and representative users. Remove criteria that prompts cannot elicit fairly.
2. Collect consented responses across modes, prompt families, lengths, language varieties and relevant access conditions. Have at least two trained independent raters use the original anchors; adjudicate a separate reference set. Choose sample sizes for the precision and subgroup comparisons needed, not an arbitrary universal number.
3. Separate participants and prompt families across training/calibration and held-out evaluation. Linked retries must not leak across those partitions.
4. Report ordinal agreement and error, human–human agreement, uncertainty intervals, abstention coverage and subgroup error patterns. Correlation alone cannot establish acceptable agreement. Check concise good responses, repetitive long responses, persuasive but unsupported claims and opposing valid positions.
5. Test evidence integrity and adversarial instructions separately from psychometric performance. Version and rerun evaluations after model, prompt or rubric changes.
6. A claim of **improved skill** needs evidence on subsequent, independently scored transfer tasks, ideally with a comparison condition. More app use, higher retry scores or user satisfaction alone do not establish learning gains.

Until that work is complete, avoid calling ratings validated, treating different modes as interchangeable scores, plotting unvalidated numbers as ability growth, or presenting a student's result as an official qualification.

## Reuse and attribution checks

Public access to a rubric PDF does not establish permission to reproduce it in a commercial app. This review recommends original concise anchors, factual descriptions of research foundations and links, not copied rubric tables, test items, logos or claims of endorsement.

| Owner | Verified terms and implementation consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Council of Europe | Its permissions page generally requires prior written permission for reproduction/republishing, with specified exceptions; the phonology report's notice allows extracts for noncommercial use with acknowledgement. References and links differ from republishing tables. Check permission before commercial reproduction or close adaptation. [Council permissions](https://www.coe.int/it/web/portal/copyright-licensing-permissions). |
| ETS | General terms restrict commercial and derivative uses without express written permission. Do not assume an available speaking-rubric PDF licenses incorporation into software. Use original anchors and factual research attribution; obtain permission for protected materials. [ETS terms](https://www.ets.org/legal/terms.html). |
| Cambridge | No affirmative commercial reuse licence for the assessed guide was verified in this review. Do not reproduce its performance descriptors or imply Cambridge accreditation without resolving permission. |
| AAC&U | The official permissions pages could not be retrieved. Accessible university-hosted original documents are not a commercial licence. Confirm current software/adaptation terms directly with AAC&U before incorporating protected wording; do not treat faculty-developed criteria as public domain. |
| OECD | Current general terms permit qualifying pre-July-2024 content reuse/adaptation under conditions, including attribution and the specified adaptation notice; however, the 2019 framework PDF carries an older notice directing commercial-use requests to OECD. Resolve the publication-specific position before shipping a reproduced/adapted rubric. Do not automatically label this 2019 PDF CC BY 4.0, and exclude third-party illustrations/logos. [Current OECD terms](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/terms-conditions.html), [framework copyright page](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2019/04/pisa-2018-assessment-and-analytical-framework_d1c359c7/b25efab8-en.pdf). |

This is a rights-check record, not a legal opinion. Scientific attribution and permission to copy protected expression are separate questions.

## Product claim to use now

Suggested wording: **“Research-informed feedback on this response. The app's ratings have not been validated as a measure of ability.”** For local mode: **“Coaching prompts only; no scores.”** For Read & Reflect: **“Based on the supplied summary and your answer.”** For all current recording workflows: **“Audio delivery is not assessed.”**

No live-provider validation was performed during this research. A configured LLM can follow the proposed rubric, but successful execution would not establish scientific validity.
